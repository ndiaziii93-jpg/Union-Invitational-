A+CThe pine cover

Your pick: A's green rail down the left with gold on where you are, and C's photograph across the top of every screen with the headline written on it, the first row of cards rising into the picture. These three frames are live: click You, Season or Vegas HQ in the rail of any of them. The gold mark moves, the photograph crossfades, the headline and then the cards rise in order; the rail, the buttons and the rows answer the pointer.

How it moves

  • Navigation: the gold mark slides to the open tab; the cover keeps the last photograph underneath and fades it out over half a second while the new headline rises.
  • Arrival: the cards rise in order, 70 ms apart, so the eye is led from the cover down the page.
  • Pointer: rail items brighten and the icon nudges right, buttons lift a pixel, table rows and lines tint on hover, the lifted cards rise a little further. Keyboard focus shows a gold ring. All of it is off for anyone who has asked their system for reduced motion.

What it keeps from A

  • The pine rail with the gold edge and the gold mark on the open tab; the crest, the books, Manage and your name at its foot.
  • Colour on the data only, once past the cover.

What it keeps from C

  • The photograph as the header of every screen, with the headline and the day's one button on it.
  • The first row of cards rising into the picture.

AThe pine rail

The book's green down the left with gold on where you are, the paper canvas beside it. Colour otherwise only on the data. Confident, and the closest to the phone's bar turned on its side.

Why it wins

  • It is unmistakably this book: the pine and gold of the phone's bar, the crest, nothing borrowed.
  • The rail holds everything the masthead and dateline carried, so the canvas is all content.
  • Dense enough for the commissioner, calm enough for a glance.

Where it strains

  • A dark rail next to a paper canvas is a strong edge; on a 13-inch screen it takes 16% of the width.
  • Two greens on one screen (the rail and the chart's series) need the chart's green kept to the data.

BThe ledger

No rail and no colour in the chrome at all: a line of tabs across the top, rules instead of cards, ink for the figures, and one gold mark for you. The club's annual, kept by a steady hand.

Why it wins

  • Most room for the tables, which is what a laptop is for.
  • Nothing dates: it is type, rules and numbers.
  • Prints as it stands.

Where it strains

  • The least like the phone; someone moving between the two notices.
  • Quiet to a fault for the fun parts: a crowned stop should feel like something.

CThe cover

The phone's Today opens on a photograph with the day set over it. This does the same on a laptop: a cover across the top of every screen carrying the question, the cards rising into it, a paper rail at the side.

Why it wins

  • The most atmosphere: the trip is a place, and the place is on screen.
  • Continuous with the phone's cover, so it reads as the same book grown up.
  • The headline figure is the cover's own line, read before anything else.

Where it strains

  • The cover costs 290px before the first row; on a small laptop the tables start lower.
  • Needs a photograph per season, and a good one.

The pick is A + C.
