Comments and bets for the gallery

Two ideas to give the people who aren’t playing a way in. Three designs for each, as live phones: type a comment, tap a reaction, put a bet on. Nothing is in the app yet. The developer notes at the bottom cover how either would be built and what it needs from you.

Comments

Three ways in
1The Round ChatComments
1

The Round Chat

One running conversation per round, like a group chat with the score pinned above it.

  • Anyone can post: pick your name once, as in My round.
  • Reactions on every message.
  • The book drops in a line when something happens: a birdie, the turn, a group finishing.
Developer’s takeThe easiest to understand and to build. Can get busy, but the score at the top keeps it on the golf.
2Hole by HoleComments
2

Hole by Hole

Comments pinned to a hole, beside that hole’s scores.

  • Red counts on the hole numbers show where the talk is.
  • Each hole shows who made what, then the comments on it.
  • The golfers see the count on Score Entry, and can read it after.
Developer’s takeThe most golf-shaped, and makes a great record of the round. A little more to learn for a non-golfer.
3The Heckle FeedComments
3

The Heckle Feed

A live feed the book writes itself from the scores, which people react to and heckle.

  • Every birdie, double, pin and drive becomes a card.
  • One tap to react: 🔥 👏 😬 🍺.
  • A short heckle under any card.
Developer’s takeThe most fun for people who don’t follow the golf closely: the book tells them what to react to. Fed by the scores already being saved, so no extra work for the refs.

Bets

Built the way FanDuel and Kalshi work · beers and shots, never money
AThe BookBets · like FanDuel
A

The Book

Works like FanDuel: a board of golf markets with American odds, a bet slip, parlays, cash out.

  • Golf’s real markets: round winner, MVP outright and top 3, matchups with a stroke handicap, three-balls, hole props, hole in one.
  • Tap odds to add them to the Betslip. Singles or Parlay, a stake in 🍺, “to win” shown before you place it.
  • A profit boost each day, live odds that move with the scores, and My Bets with each leg ticking off and a Cash out offer.
Developer’s takeThe book is the house: it sets the odds from the live standings and pays out, so every bet is accepted at once. Closest to FanDuel, and the one golfers will know.
BThe ExchangeBets · like Kalshi
B

The Exchange

Works like Kalshi: yes/no questions, each priced 1–99¢, paying a full 🍺 a contract if you’re right.

  • The price is the chance: Yes at 62¢ means the group thinks 62%. Yes and No always add up to a dollar of beer.
  • Tap a market for its chart, order book, and an order ticket: Buy or Sell, Yes or No, Market or Limit.
  • Sell any time before it settles to lock in a gain. Every market states its rules: what counts, and when it closes.
Developer’s takePeople trade with each other, so prices are the group’s real view. With 23 people the book quietly offers both sides too, so an order never sits unfilled.
CThe Union BookBets · both in one
C

The Union Book

Both in one tab: the book for the golf, the exchange for everything else, and challenges between friends.

  • Board: FanDuel-style odds on the rounds.
  • Markets: Kalshi-style yes/no on the week (“Will anyone hit the water on 17?”).
  • Challenges and My Bets, with The Tab of who owes whom at the bar.
Developer’s takeThe most complete, and the most to explain. Odds suit the golfers; percentages suit everyone else. One balance of 🍺 across all three.

Who can post and bet

  • No PIN. People tap their name once, from the trip roster (golfers, officials, spectators), and the phone remembers them, just as My round does now.
  • The commissioner can hide any comment, and void any bet.
  • Golfers can bet on themselves, never against themselves.

How it’s built

  • Each comment and each bet is its own small record, so two people posting at once never overwrite each other, the same way hearts work on photos.
  • Bets settle themselves from the scores the refs already save. Nobody has to mark a bet won.
  • Works with no signal: posts wait on the phone and go up when it’s back, like photos.
  • One setup step for you: the database only accepts record types it already knows. Adding “comments” and “bets” is a one-line change I’d write for you to paste into Supabase’s SQL editor. No keys or passwords involved.

How FanDuel and Kalshi work, and what was kept

  • FanDuel is a sportsbook: the house sets American odds (−250 favourite, +300 outsider), bets go on a Betslip as singles or a parlay (every leg must win), and open bets can be cashed out early for a smaller sum. Golf markets include outright winner, top-finish places with dead-heat rules, matchups with a stroke handicap, three-balls, first-round leader and hole props.
  • Kalshi is an exchange: every market is a yes/no contract priced 1–99¢ that pays $1 if right; Yes and No add up to a dollar; people trade with each other on an order book with market or limit orders, and can sell before it settles. Each market publishes its settlement rules.
  • All of that is here, with 🍺 instead of dollars and the book’s own scores as the settlement source.

Beers, not money

  • Everyone starts each day with 10 🍺 to bet. Shots are worth 2 🍺. On the exchange a contract costs 1–99¢ of a beer and pays a whole 🍺 if right.
  • Winnings show on The Tab: who owes whom, settled at the bar.
  • A leaderboard of the best tipster of the week, for the closing ceremony.
  • Betting on a round closes when its first group tees off; hole bets close as that hole starts.

My recommendation

  • Comments: the Heckle Feed (3), with a chat box under it. The book does the work of telling non-golfers what just happened, and they react.
  • Bets: the Union Book (C). FanDuel’s board for the golf, where the golfers already think in odds; Kalshi’s yes/no markets for everyone else, where a percentage needs no explaining; and challenges for the bar. One 🍺 balance, one My Bets.
  • Both would live under Boards, as “Chat” and “Bets”, next to Photos.
