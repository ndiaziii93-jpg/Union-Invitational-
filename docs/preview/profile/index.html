The player card, three ways

Three takes on the same player, before any of it goes into the app. Pick one, or take the parts you like from each.

Everything here is worked out, not invented. The gross scores behind these numbers are made up — six pretend rounds at Leatherhead off the White tees. Every figure on the cards is then derived from them by the book’s own functions: the same Stableford, the same stroke allocation, the same Union handicap. So what you are judging is what the app can actually say about somebody from the cards it already keeps, with nobody typing a single extra number.

Where it would live

On the Season tab, behind a player’s name on the standings and the Order of Merit — tap a name, get their card. The same card is what you would hold up next to somebody else’s before a stop opens.

Nothing here needs a new screen on a phone: each of these is drawn at 380px, which is a phone’s width with the page’s own margins taken off.

What I would still want to settle

Three things, whichever one you pick:

Sample size. Six cards is enough for this to mean something; two is not. I would hold the attributes back until three finished cards are in and say so plainly rather than print a number that is noise.
Who sees it. Everybody’s card visible to everybody, or your own card plus a thinner public version? A weakness is funny between mates and less funny on a page anyone can open.
How sharp the voice is. The verdict lines below are mine. They can be drier, or a good deal ruder.
