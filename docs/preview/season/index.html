Trade-offs

For
  • One app, one habit: the Vegas week is the same screens people used in Belek
  • Every virtual card is a real card: photos, marks, the report, the manual
  • Belek stays readable in place, one switch away
Against
  • The biggest build: the book has to carry two seasons and know the time of year
  • A tab that changes meaning by season needs care so Today never confuses

B · The Clubhouse in front. A new front door for the year: five tiles. The tournament book stays exactly as it is, opened from the Clubhouse during Vegas week and reached from a tile as the Belek archive the rest of the time. Off-season life, sign-ups, money, games and handicaps live in the Clubhouse. The lighter build.

Trade-offs

For
  • The tournament book is untouched and proven; nothing in it can break
  • Fast to build: new pages on top, one new table underneath
  • Belek is a tile, not a mode
Against
  • Two places to be: the Clubhouse for the year, the book for the week
  • Virtual cards use a copy of the scoring screen, so the two can drift
  • Handicaps have to be carried into the book by hand at Vegas

C · Two books, one roster. A second book for Vegas 2027 at its own address, built from the same code, with its own database tables. The Belek book stays where it is, read-only. The roster, the Union handicaps and the virtual games are born in the Vegas book, and the two link to each other. The cleanest separation.

For
