A chat button people will actually use

Three ways to put the chat one tap away from every screen, without another tab. Each phone is live: tap the chat way in, type, post. Tap above the chat or swipe it down to close it. The ▶ Someone posts button under each phone shows what happens when a new message or a birdie lands.

AThe bubbleFloats over every screen

A round chat button in the corner of every screen, like Messenger’s. A red count of what you haven’t read, and the newest line slides out beside it for a few seconds.

  • Always there, never in the way: it covers nothing you need.
  • The red count and the slide-out are what get people tapping.
  • One tap: the chat rises with the keyboard already up.
Developer’s takeThe familiar one, and the cheapest on screen space. It hides on Score Entry, so a ref with a putter in one hand never hits it by mistake.
BThe lineA message box above the bar

A slim “Say something to the group…” box sits just above the bottom bar on every screen, with the latest line over it.

  • The strongest invitation to type: it already looks like a box to write in.
  • You can read the conversation without opening anything.
  • Tap it and you’re typing.
Developer’s takeThe most used, and the most room taken: about a thumb’s height off every screen. Also hidden on Score Entry.
CThe tickerA live strip at the top

A live strip under the header turns over the latest birdies, splashes and messages, with a “Join in” button. A chat button with a count sits by the title.

  • Feels like a TV broadcast: it makes the round feel live.
  • Shows the book’s moments, so there’s always something to react to.
  • Nothing at the bottom of the screen at all.
Developer’s takeThe most atmosphere, and nothing in the thumb’s way. It scrolls away with the page, so it is strongest on Today and Boards.

The same in all three

  • The chat opens as a panel from the bottom (three quarters of the screen, like the resort cards), on today’s round, with the keyboard up and the box ready.
  • Swipe it down or tap above it to close; you’re back exactly where you were.
  • The book’s moments (birdies, water, the turn) are in it, with reactions and heckles, as now. The Chat tab on Boards stays too.
  • The unread count is kept on each phone, so nobody else can see what you haven’t read.

My recommendation: A, the bubble

  • It’s on every screen without costing any room, so Scores, Bets and the resort guide stay as they are.
  • The red count and the slide-out do the persuading: people open what has a number on it.
  • If you want it pushed harder, B is the one; C is the most fun but the easiest to scroll past.
