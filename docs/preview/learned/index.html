What the other books do

TAG Heuer Golf and Golf GameBook, read for how they are built and how they treat a golfer — then held against our own book. Ideas, not assets.

Where this came from Their own published material, not their software: TAG Heuer Golf's user guide, Golf GameBook's product pages and tournament manager, their store listings and support notes. No binaries were taken apart, and nothing of theirs is in our repo.
The honest frame

We are not building either of these

GameBook has two million golfers, forty-two thousand courses and a GPS rangefinder. TAG Heuer has a watch on your wrist detecting your swing. We have fifteen people and one trip a year. Most of what they do is answering a problem we do not have — so the useful reading is not “what do they have that we lack”, it is what did they learn about golfers that we can use. That list is short, and three of the five things on it we are already most of the way to.

What they know

Six things worth taking seriously

The leaderboard is the product, not the scorecard

GameBook

What they do

Their whole pitch is watching other people play: “live leaderboards place you in the middle of the action even from the comfort of your couch.” You can follow a round you are not in, like somebody's scorecard, and comment on it hole by hole while it is happening.

Why it works

Golf is four hours alone with your own mistakes. The app's answer is that somebody is watching — and the people watching are having a better time than the scorer is. The scorecard is the excuse; the audience is the product.

Take itWe have boards, and a push when somebody tees off. What we do not have is a way to watch a round that is happening. For a group spread across England and California, in wildly different time zones, that is the single biggest thing on this page.

The Driving Zone

TAG Heuer

What they do

Your tee shots drawn on the hole itself: today's in white, every previous round's in colour. One picture of where you actually hit it from this tee, over years.

Why it works

It answers a question a golfer genuinely has and cannot otherwise answer — “do I always miss this one right?” — without a word of analysis. The pattern is the insight.

Take itWe already built the two halves: shots tapped onto a 3D hole, and a card history per player. This is joining them up. It is the best idea on this page and the cheapest one we could build, because nothing new has to be invented.

Gross and putts, not just gross

TAG Heuer

What they do

Scoring asks for two numbers on every hole: the gross and the number of putts. One extra tap.

Why it works

Putts are half of golf and the half nobody remembers accurately. Two numbers instead of one turns a scorecard into a diagnosis for the price of a single tap.

Take itOur report card already grades putting — it gave Norberto a B — but it infers it. One more tap per hole and that grade stops being a guess. It also feeds the practice manual, which already picks drills by the department you score worst in.

Twenty formats, because a society never plays one game

GameBook

What they do

Skins, match play, scramble, better ball, stableford, Irish rumble, and a Ryder-style “Reds vs Blues” team match play with its own tournament type.

Why it works

The format is the entertainment. The same four players and the same eighteen holes feel like a different day because the bet changed.

Mostly have itBelek already ran better-ball, a championship final and squads. Stops are stableford. The one obvious gap is skins — a hole-by-hole pot that carries over — which is the format a group of fifteen plays when nothing else is on.

The eclectic card

GameBook

What they do

Your best score on each hole, taken across every round you have ever played there, assembled into one imaginary perfect card.

Why it works

It is the only golf statistic that is pure pleasure. Nobody shot it, everybody could have, and it gets better forever. It also gives a reason to play the same course again.

Take itWe have every card a player has posted and the holes they were posted on. This is a page, not a feature — and for a book built around home courses played all season, it is exactly the right kind of vanity.

Distance rings, and a target you can move

TAG Heuer

What they do

The hole map is ruled with lines every ten yards, and you can drag a target anywhere on it to ask what the carry is.

Why it works

It turns a picture into an instrument. You stop looking at the hole and start measuring it.

Leave itTheirs is GPS on a real tee with the ball at your feet. Ours is a hole drawn from a card, played a day later from a sofa — rings on it would be precision we do not have and would be lying about. The yardage pins we already draw are honest; rings would not be.

And one thing we do that they do not

Neither app knows what a trip is. GameBook manages a tournament; TAG Heuer manages a swing. Nothing in either of them holds a room list, a deposit, a flight that lands at 15:40, a shirt size, or the fact that Letty is now coming for four nights instead of seven. Vegas HQ is not a feature either of them has thought about, because neither of them is a book for one group of friends who go somewhere together once a year. That is the part worth protecting.
