Your shuttle: three ways to keep it small

Each phone below is Today on Sunday, the day before you land. The shuttle sits small under the countdown; tap it to see everything: when it leaves, where to meet, whose flight it waits for, and who rides with you. Tap again to fold it away.

Sample flights and times only. Nobody’s real travel is on this page.

ABoarding passA ticket stub that tears open

A slim stub with the route, your landing time and who’s riding as little faces. Tap and it opens below the perforation, like tearing off a boarding pass.

  • Reads at a glance: where, when, how many.
  • Opens in place, so the page doesn’t jump.
  • The most “travel” of the three.
Feels likeWallet and airline boarding passes.
BLive pillOne line, a sheet on tap

One dark pill: “Your shuttle · tomorrow · waits for XQ 200, 6:05 PM”, with a red pulse on the day. Tap and the full card rises from the bottom, like the chat.

  • The smallest: never pushes the scores down.
  • The detail gets the whole screen when you want it.
  • Matches how the chat already opens.
Feels likeAn iPhone Live Activity or an Uber trip in progress.
CDepartures boardSplit-flap letters, airport style

Your shuttle as a line on an airport board: split-flap letters, a blinking status. Tap and the board drops down with a row per flight: time, flight, who’s on it, and its status.

  • The boldest look on the page, so nobody misses it.
  • The flight rows read like a real arrivals board.
  • The darkest and heaviest of the three.
Feels likeStanding under the arrivals board at Antalya.

My pick: A, the boarding pass

  • It holds the three things you check most (where, when, who) without opening it.
  • It opens where it is, so Today doesn’t jump or cover the scores.
  • It looks like travel at a glance, and still sits in the book’s own paper and ink.

The same in all three

  • It appears three days before you land, and the day before you fly home.
  • Opened, it shows the same detail as now: pickup, meeting point, whose flight it waits for, riders by flight.
  • It remembers whether you left it open.
  • The bleachers never see it.
