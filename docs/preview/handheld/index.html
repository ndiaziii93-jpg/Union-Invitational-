What we learnediPhoneiPad

Six things the laptop taught, applied in line.

  • 1 · The headline answers the question

    "Morning, Norberto III." becomes "Morning, Norberto. Your card is in: 34 points, 2nd in the Frostbite Open." The first line is the news; the sub-line is the facts.

  • 2 · The photograph under the banner

    The week's Today opens on a picture; the Clubhouse does not. Each screen gets a short cover beneath the rule with its headline on it, and the first card rises into the picture.

  • 3 · Figures as type, not rings

    The three dials become three figures on a rule: Form 16, Played 2 of 2, Ready 50%. Bigger numbers, less chrome, and the colour goes back to the data.

  • 4 · Colour on the data

    The open stop's gold slab becomes a paper card with a gold kicker; the pine plate on Season becomes the cover's own headline. Green and red mean good and outstanding, and nothing else.

  • 5 · Tables as rules and a tint

    Your row on a board is a soft tint with a gold edge, not green bars between the cells. Headers are small caps on a single ink rule.

  • 6 · Motion

    The gold mark slides along the tab bar; the cover crossfades; the headline, then the cards, rise in order. Chips and buttons press. All off under reduced motion.
