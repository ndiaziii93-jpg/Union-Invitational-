Ref tools: three ways each

Shots to the green, and putts

Count mode already counts every shot. These split the count in two, before the green and on it, so the card knows the putts too. The total is still the score, three putts still mark themselves, and the recap can say who putted well.

1ATwo halves a tileTop half a shot, bottom half a putt

Each golfer's tile is split: tap the top for a shot to the green, the bottom for a putt. The score is the two added up.

  • One tap a shot, whichever kind: no mode to remember.
  • Shots and putts side by side, at a glance.
  • Smaller targets than one big tile.
1B"On the green" switchOne big tile, flipped at the green

The big tile as now. When a golfer's ball is on the green, tap On the green and every tap after counts as a putt. Shots are dots, putts are rings.

  • Keeps the biggest target for every tap.
  • One extra tap a golfer a hole.
  • Forget to flip it and the putts count as shots.
1CScore, then puttsNo live counting: two numbers a hole

No counting as you go. When the hole's done, the ref puts in the score as now, then taps the number of putts on a row under it.

  • The least to do while walking.
  • Works with the keypad the refs already know.
  • Relies on remembering the putts at the end of the hole.

Shot of the day

A gold ★ Shot of the day button on every golfer, for the refs: tap it, say what happened, and it's nominated. A ref can nominate as many as they like through the round. After the round, the refs and the rest of the group go through them together and vote for the one. The winner goes into the recap.

2AOne vote eachEveryone votes, most votes wins

Nominate on the course. After the round, the Shot of the day board lists every nomination with what the ref wrote. Everyone, refs and group, gets one vote; the most votes wins, and the commissioner can call a tie.

  • Simplest: one list, one vote.
  • The whole trip has a say.
  • A popular golfer can win on friends' votes.
2BRefs shortlist, group votesTwo stages

First the refs go through every nomination and pick a shortlist of three. Then the group votes on those three. The refs keep the quality up; the group crowns it.

  • The refs, who saw the shots, filter the list.
  • The group still decides.
  • One more step after the round.
2CWeighted votesA ref's vote counts double

One list and one vote each, as in A, but a ref's vote counts twice. The bars show the running count live; the commissioner closes the vote.

  • One step, like A.
  • The refs' eye counts for more, without a separate shortlist.
  • Needs explaining once.

The score pad: no more walking on

Today the pad jumps to the next golfer by itself after each score. The refs want it to close once a score is picked, and to choose the next golfer themselves.

3APick and closeExactly as asked

Tap a golfer's box, tap the number, and the pad closes. Back on the list, tap whoever is next.

  • Nothing moves on its own.
  • Two taps a golfer.
  • Nothing points at who is still to go.
3BPick, close, and a nudgeThe next empty golfer glows

As A, but once the pad closes the next golfer without a score glows gold. Nothing opens by itself; the glow is just a reminder.

  • The ref chooses, as asked.
  • Hard to miss a golfer.
  • Still two taps a golfer.
3CNumbers on the rowNo pad at all

No pad. Each golfer's row opens a strip of numbers under their name; tap one and the row folds back to show the score. Tap the score to change it.

  • Everything stays on one screen.
  • Mulligans and the pick-up question still pop up when needed.
  • Smaller number buttons than the pad's.

My picks: 1A, 2B, 3B

  • 1A: one tap a shot with nothing to remember to switch, and the putts come for free. 1B saves space but a forgotten switch counts putts as shots.
  • 2B: the refs saw the shots, so they pick the shortlist; the group still crowns it. 2A is the simplest if you'd rather everyone just votes.
  • 3B: the ref chooses who's next, as asked, and the glow stops anyone being missed.
