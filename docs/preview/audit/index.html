Targets you have to aim at, while holding a club

Apple's own floor is 44 points. Six controls are under it — and the worst of them are the ones you press standing on a tee.

Worth doing

.hcell 34×40, the hole strip, 36 of them on the scoring screen · .h3club 40×32, the club chips on the hole · .ehd-art 39×34, “See the hole” · .h3big 130×29, “Open it full screen” · .moonbtn 32×32 on a phone · .ts-ask 40×40

Way oneGrow the hit area, not the ink

Keep every one of them looking exactly as it does and hang an invisible 44-point pad off it. Nothing moves, nothing is redrawn, and the strip still fits eighteen holes across a phone.

Cheapest. No visual change at all.

Way twoMake the ones you use playing genuinely bigger

The hole strip and the club chips grow to 44, and the strip shows fewer holes at a time, scrolling as it already does. Honest, and it admits the screen is used outdoors.

Changes how the scoring screen looks.

Way threeA glove mode

One switch in your own screen that grows every target in the book to 44 and keeps it there. Belek was cold; Vegas in November is not warm at seven in the morning.

Dearest, and a setting nobody may find.
2

A button with no name

The “ask” control carries no label of any kind — a screen reader announces it as “button”, and nothing else in the book can describe it either.

Worth doing

act=askOpen · .ts-ask · 40×40 · no text, no aria-label · on every screen

Way oneGive it the name it already has

One attribute: “Ask about the rules”. It is a one-line change and it is simply missing.

Minutes. No argument against it.

Way twoName it and audit every other control at once

The agent already checks this on every crawl. Make an unnamed control fail the suite, so the next one never ships.

An hour, and it stays fixed.

Way threeGive it a word on the glass

Not just a label for the reader: put “Rules” under the icon. Nobody has to guess what it does, and the target gets bigger for free.

Takes room on a phone.
3

Nine ways to Vegas HQ on one screen

The You tab offers the same destination nine times. Each one made sense when it was added; together they read as a page that does not know what it wants you to do.

Judgement

act=go a=hq × 9, all on the You tab — the cover, the line panel, the joining card, and six more

Way oneCut it to two

One on the cover, one at the end of your Vegas line. Everything else loses its button and keeps its words.

Quick. Somebody will miss a shortcut.

Way twoOne door per panel, and only where the panel is about HQ

A panel that talks about money, rooms or the sign-up gets a door; a panel about your round does not. The rule is stateable, so it stays true as panels are added.

An afternoon, and it holds.

Way threeLeave it, and make the agent watch the number

Nine is not hurting anybody today. Set the ceiling at nine and let the crawl shout when a tenth appears.

Free. Fixes nothing.
4

“Choose” — twice, and neither one opens

On the week's entry setup there are two identical “Choose” controls for closest-to-the-pin, and tapping either changes nothing on screen. This is my best candidate for a real defect; I have not confirmed it is not simply waiting for data my fixture lacks.

Needs a look

act=pickOpen · .pkf · “? Not chosen yet · Choose” · 2 identical controls · nothing happened on tap · at entry » setup

Way oneFind out first

Half an hour with a real week fixture — rounds, pairs, a field — to see whether it is broken or just idle. Everything else here is guessing until that is known.

Half an hour. Do this one first.

Way twoMake it say why it cannot open

If it is idle because nothing is nominated yet, it should say so instead of looking pressable. A control that cannot act should not look like one that can.

Small, once the cause is known.

Way threeGive the agent a week to walk

Build a full week fixture — three rounds, pairings, cards — and let the crawl run against it. This one would have been answered already, and so would the next twenty.

A day. Pays for itself.
5

Post, with nothing to post

The chat's Post button is pressable when the box is empty. It does nothing and says nothing, which reads as a broken button rather than an empty box.

Judgement

act=chatSend · “Post” · nothing happened · at today » chat

Way oneGrey it out until there are words

The ordinary answer, and the one nobody has to learn. The button says what it can do before it is pressed.

Minutes.

Way twoLeave it live and say why

“Write something first”, under the box. Better for a thumb that has already committed, worse for a thumb that has not.

Minutes. A matter of taste.

Way threeFix the whole family

Every Save, Post and Send in the book, one rule: a control that cannot act is disabled and explains itself. The agent counts them and holds the line.

A day. Ends the class.
6

The book's second ink is just under the line, in daylight

Night is clean. Day has 23 places where the secondary text sits between 3.78 and 4.14 against cream, where the standard asks 4.5. It is the same colour every time.

Judgement

--turf #5C6B55 → 4.14 on paper · 3.78 on sunk · 4.65 on page · needs 4.5
A 12% darkening to #515E4B clears every surface: 5.00 · 4.57 · 5.61. Same hue, same character.

Way oneDarken the one token

Every secondary line in the book gets a shade deeper and clears the standard. One value, same hue, nothing else moves. Reversible in a second if you hate it.

One line. Changes the whole book, slightly.

Way twoOnly where it actually fails

Darken it on the sunk and paper surfaces and leave it alone on the page. Surgical, and a second rule to remember forever.

Fiddly. Two truths about one colour.

Way threeDecide it is fine and write that down

4.14 is close, it is the book's established look, and nobody has complained. Record the exception so the test stops raising it and a real regression still would.

Free. An honest decision, not a dodge.
7

Pressing what you already chose says nothing at all

Fifteen controls do nothing when tapped because they are already the answer — the package you bought, the tab you are on, the club already selected. Correct, and silent. On a phone, silence and broken look identical.

Judgement

15 controls · signPkg, joinGolf, joinStay, shotClub, stopPick, clubMTab, packKind and nine more

Way oneA flicker on the press

Any already-chosen control gives the same small pulse when pressed. One rule, every control, and it answers “did that work?” before the question is asked.

An afternoon. One rule for the lot.

Way twoLeave them, fix the ones you press while playing

Nobody taps a package twice. People absolutely tap a club twice, in the rain, unsure whether the first one took. Do shotClub, clubHole and the keypad; leave the rest.

An hour, aimed where it matters.

Way threeLeave them alone

Every one of them already shows its state — the chosen chip is pine, the current tab is marked. A flicker on fifteen controls is motion the book does not currently have.

Free. Defensible.
What I would do, in order

2 (the unnamed button) and 5 (Post) are minutes each and have no argument against them. 1, way one — padding the targets without moving a pixel — is the best value in the list, because the controls it fixes are the ones held in a cold hand on a tee. 4 needs half an hour of finding out before anybody decides anything. 3, 6 and 7 are yours: they are taste, not faults, and I have given you the case for leaving each of them alone.

The one thing I would add that is not on the list: a real week fixture. Half of what the crawl could not judge, it could not judge because the book had no tournament in it. Build that and the agent gets twice as useful overnight.
