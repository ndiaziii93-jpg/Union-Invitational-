Shot data: three ways to get it

All three capture the same thing — club, where the ball finished, how far it went — and all three write the same row, so a round entered one way reads identically to a round entered another. What differs is what the golfer has to do while they are stood there with a club in their hand.

The hole below is the real 1st at Leatherhead, drawn from the club's own map — the same geometry the app's 3D view already uses. Tap through all three, then tell me which to build.

Way one

Tap the hole

Your idea. Pick the club, tap where it finished.

Hole 1Par 5 · 483 yds

A played hole, already on the card. Press Clear, then pick a club and tap the ground yourself.

What it is good at
Fast and obvious. You already know where your ball went — pointing at it needs no thought. Gives true coordinates, so every number downstream (distance, dispersion, proximity) is real.
What it is bad at
You are aiming at a drawing. A tap is accurate to maybe five yards on a 480-yard hole at phone size, and worse near the green where everything matters most. It also needs you to remember to do it.

Build: about two days. The hole geometry and the tap-to-ground maths already exist in the 3D view. The work is the shot layer, the club row, and the undo.

Way two

Say what happened

No map. Club, where it ended up, how far is left.

What it is good at
Works on any phone, in any light, with no signal and no 3D. Three taps a shot and you are done. It is also the only one that still works when you are writing the round up afterwards in the bar.
What it is bad at
No spatial truth. "Right rough, 150 left" cannot tell you how far right, so you get club distances and a miss bias but never a real dispersion pattern. The yardage is whatever the golfer guesses.

Build: about a day. It is chips and a number — no geometry at all.

Way three

Walk it

The phone knows where you are. Stand at the ball, tap once.

Hole 1Par 5 · 483 yds

Simulated: each tap walks you down the hole the way a round actually plays.

What it is good at
The most accurate by a long way, and the least work while playing: one tap at the ball, no aiming. The app already takes a GPS fix on the hole, so the hard part is done. Distances are measured, not guessed.
What it is bad at
A phone's fix is good to three to ten yards and worse under trees. It only works while you are actually out there — nothing can be reconstructed later. And it costs battery on a four-hour round.

Build: about three days. The fix exists; the work is averaging a stable reading, an accuracy ring, and the "that reading was rubbish, let me drag it" correction.

What I would do

Build one data shape, and let the way in change

These are not really three products. A shot is { hole, n, club, x, y, lie } in every one of them — only x, y is sometimes missing. So the thing to decide first is not which screen to build, it is that all three write the same row. Then the way in can change without a migration, and a golfer can use whichever suits the moment: GPS on the course, the map when the fix is poor, words when they are filling it in from memory.

If you want one to start with, I would build way one first and make way three an upgrade to it, with way two as the fallback that never fails. In that order:

  1. Tap the hole. It is the cheapest real data you can get, it needs nothing from the network, and it is the screen the other two hang off. The 3D hole is already drawn; this adds a layer on top of it.
  2. Walk it. Once the shot layer exists, GPS just fills it in automatically — same shots, less typing. The tap stays as the correction when the fix is bad, which is the pattern every serious golf app uses.
  3. Say it. One evening's work, and it is the honest answer for anyone who forgets, or plays somewhere the hole is not drawn.

What it unlocks, in order of how soon it is worth anything: club distances (after about three rounds), miss bias — which way you actually leak it (five rounds), approach proximity (ten), and eventually strokes gained against the group's own baseline rather than a tour average, which is the number that would actually settle arguments in Las Vegas.

One warning worth hearing now. Shot capture is the feature golfers love in the shop and abandon on the 4th tee. Whichever we build, it has to survive being ignored: a round with shots on three holes and nothing on the rest must still post, still count for the handicap, and never nag. I would build it that way from the first commit rather than bolt it on.
