A · The Tour

The year is a tour. Eleven stops from December to October, each a named event with its own trophy, format and window. Cards land, the board moves live, and at the window’s close the stop crowns its winner and the Register writes it up. Points go to an Order of Merit, and Vegas is the Tour Championship.

  • Stop: live board, cards in, the rules, enter my card.
  • Tour: the Order of Merit, the trophy cabinet, the awards.
  • Card: your Union handicap, form, trophies, the manual.
  • Vegas HQ: countdown, the field, money, rooms, flights, vendors, exports.

Twelve people understand a tour at once: a stop, a leader, a champion. Every card is a real card, so the report and the manual refresh with it. One set of habits all year, and Vegas is simply the last stop.

The biggest build of the three. Planning lives in one tab of a golf app, so money and flights sit behind the leaderboard. The points table needs explaining once, in December, and then it runs itself.

B · The Clubhouse

The app opens on you. Three dials: your form, which is your Union handicap and its trend; how many of the season’s games you have played; and how ready you are for Vegas, from six things only you can settle. Under them, the one thing to do next. The group lives one tab over, in the standings and the field.

  • You: the dials, the open game, your next task, your manual.
  • Play: enter a card with its photo, your approved course, the open games.
  • Standings: this month, the season, the handicaps, the awards.
  • Vegas: your line and the field’s readiness; Locker: report, trophies, photos.

Nobody is chased. Each person sees their own gaps and the app nudges them: deposits in seven days, no card from you, your flights are missing. Readiness rolls up for the commissioner without a spreadsheet.

Less theatre. A tour crowns a champion in public; a clubhouse keeps the score quietly. The monthly winner needs its own moment on the standings tab so the games still feel like events.

C · The Register

Built from the commissioner’s chair. The Board is a live wall: twelve tiles with four lights each, the money collected, the rooms settled, a ticker of what closes next. Games end on a siren that crowns the winner, writes the recap, sends the dispatch to the group and queues the post. The Desk runs the reminders, the approvals, the rooms and the exports.

  • Board: the field at a glance, money, rooms, the next thing that closes.
  • Season: fixtures, results, the table, monthly and season awards.
  • Stop: the live board, then the siren and the crowning.
  • Desk: reminders, course approvals, rooms planner, flights, exports.

It runs the year for you. Reminders go out on a schedule, the siren does the crowning and the writing, and the vendor gets a file rather than a message. The Board replaces the spreadsheet in one screen.

It leads with the commissioner’s view, so a golfer opening it on a Tuesday sees the field before their own game. The golfer’s side needs the same care as the Board or it reads as a control room.

What is already decided

Money

Everyone in the group, as values: the budget, what is paid and what is outstanding for each person, with the deadline on top. Nothing is collected in the app; Zelle and the portal stay where they are. The social accounts never get it.

The card

Eighteen holes entered in the app inside the stop’s window, on a course the commissioner has approved, with a photograph of the signed card attached before it posts. The master PIN can correct one afterwards.

Handicaps

The Union handicap, worked out by the app from the best three of your last six virtual rounds against your course’s par and slope. No official index is needed. The Vegas bands are locked from it on 13 October.

Partners

Yes, everyone travelling: a line each for rooms, shirts, status and flights, and their own events in Vegas, the women’s 18-hole round and the partners’ 9, scored like any other round.

Vendors

Three exports from the roster, one tap each: headcount and names with seven or five days, rooms with type and nights, and the flight manifest with numbers and landing times. A PDF to send or a CSV to paste.

Belek

It is frozen and stays readable: the scores, the recaps, the photos and the report as they were on 2 November. It is the first season in the archive, with the UK edition as a line of text before it.
