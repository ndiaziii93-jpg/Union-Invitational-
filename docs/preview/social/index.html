Comments and bets for the gallery

Two ideas to give the people who aren’t playing a way in. Three designs for each, as live phones: type a comment, tap a reaction, put a bet on. Nothing is in the app yet. The developer notes at the bottom cover how either would be built and what it needs from you.

Comments

Three ways in
1

The Round Chat

One running conversation per round, like a group chat with the score pinned above it.

  • Anyone can post: pick your name once, as in My round.
  • Reactions on every message.
  • The book drops in a line when something happens: a birdie, the turn, a group finishing.
Developer’s takeThe easiest to understand and to build. Can get busy, but the score at the top keeps it on the golf.
2

Hole by Hole

Comments pinned to a hole, beside that hole’s scores.

  • Red counts on the hole numbers show where the talk is.
  • Each hole shows who made what, then the comments on it.
  • The golfers see the count on Score Entry, and can read it after.
Developer’s takeThe most golf-shaped, and makes a great record of the round. A little more to learn for a non-golfer.
3

The Heckle Feed

A live feed the book writes itself from the scores, which people react to and heckle.

  • Every birdie, double, pin and drive becomes a card.
  • One tap to react: 🔥 👏 😬 🍺.
  • A short heckle under any card.
Developer’s takeThe most fun for people who don’t follow the golf closely: the book tells them what to react to. Fed by the scores already being saved, so no extra work for the refs.

Bets

For beers and shots, never money
A

The Sportsbook

In the style of FanDuel: markets with odds, tap to add to a bet slip, stake in beers.

  • Markets: round winner, MVP, Ryder Cup, closest to the pin, holes to watch.
  • Odds move with the live scores.
  • A same-round parlay for the brave.
Developer’s takeLooks the most like the real thing, and odds are fun to read. The odds are worked out from the standings with a simple formula, not set by anyone.
B

The Market

In the style of Kalshi: yes-or-no questions with a live chance, set by how the group bets.

  • “Will Robby B win MVP?” 62% chance.
  • Buy Yes or No with beers; the price is the crowd’s view.
  • A small line shows how the chance moved through the round.
Developer’s takeThe simplest to settle: every question resolves Yes or No from the book’s own results. Percentages are easier than odds for people who don’t bet.
C

The Challenge

Friend-to-friend side bets: pick two players, a stretch of holes and the forfeit.

  • “Matt D beats Ray V on the back nine. Loser buys a round of shots.”
  • The other person accepts or declines.
  • The book settles it from the scores, and keeps The Tab of who owes what.
Developer’s takeThe most personal and the most likely to end at the bar. No odds to explain. Everything settles itself from the card.

Who can post and bet

  • No PIN. People tap their name once, from the trip roster (golfers, officials, spectators), and the phone remembers them, just as My round does now.
  • The commissioner can hide any comment, and void any bet.
  • Golfers can bet on themselves, never against themselves.

How it’s built

  • Each comment and each bet is its own small record, so two people posting at once never overwrite each other, the same way hearts work on photos.
  • Bets settle themselves from the scores the refs already save. Nobody has to mark a bet won.
  • Works with no signal: posts wait on the phone and go up when it’s back, like photos.
  • One setup step for you: the database only accepts record types it already knows. Adding “comments” and “bets” is a one-line change I’d write for you to paste into Supabase’s SQL editor. No keys or passwords involved.

Beers, not money

  • Everyone starts each day with 10 🍺 to bet. Shots are worth 2 🍺.
  • Winnings show on The Tab: who owes whom, settled at the bar.
  • A leaderboard of the best tipster of the week, for the closing ceremony.
  • Betting on a round closes when its first group tees off; hole bets close as that hole starts.

My recommendation

  • Comments: the Heckle Feed (3), with a chat box under it. The book does the work of telling non-golfers what just happened, and they react.
  • Bets: the Market (B), with Challenges (C) as a second tab. Yes/no with a percentage is the easiest to understand, settles itself, and challenges add the personal side.
  • Both would live under Boards, as “Chat” and “Bets”, next to Photos.
