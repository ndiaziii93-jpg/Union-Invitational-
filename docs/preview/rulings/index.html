Ask for a ruling: three ways in

Ray V’s tee shot on the par-3 7th has found the pond. Each phone below is a way for a ref or a player to ask about it there and then. Tap through each one: the answer comes back the way it will in the app, from the Rules of Golf and the Union’s own rules (the triple-bogey cap, the breakfast ball, one mulligan a nine, none on Sunday), with Ray’s card in mind.

The answers come from the same Claude that already writes the round recaps. It gives guidance; the refs and the commissioner make the call.

AOn the holeA ruling button on the scorecard

A small ⚖️ Ruling button beside each player on the hole being scored. It already knows the round, the hole, the par, who, and what’s left of their mulligans; pick what happened or type it.

  • Fastest in the moment: two taps from the scorecard.
  • The answer offers the next step on the card: use the mulligan, mark the water.
  • Every ruling is kept with the round, so the next group gets the same answer.
Best forThe ref with the scorecard open, standing at the pond.
BRules deskA Rulings tab beside the card

Scores gets two tabs: Card and Rulings. The desk has the common situations as tiles, a box to ask anything, and the day’s rulings log, each one stamped when a ref confirms it.

  • One place for every question, from any group.
  • The log keeps the day consistent: the same pond, the same answer.
  • Players can read it to settle an argument without a ref.
Best forA trip that wants a record, and refs who like to confirm.
CIn the chatThe Rules Official answers in the chat

A ⚖️ Ruling switch on the chat box. The question goes into the round’s chat for everyone, and the Rules Official answers there; a ref can stamp it.

  • Everyone sees it, and everyone can chime in.
  • Nothing new to learn: it’s the chat they already use.
  • Rulings mix with the banter, so they’re harder to find later.
Best forThe fun of it: the whole trip watches the ruling land.

My pick: A, with B’s log

  • Rulings happen at the ball, with the card open: the button should be right there, already knowing the hole and the player.
  • Because it knows the card, the answer can offer the fix (“use Ray’s front mulligan”) instead of just reading out the rule.
  • Keep a small rulings log on the round, so every group gets the same answer at the same pond.

What every answer gives

  • The ruling in one line, with the penalty and the Rule number.
  • The options, in order, the way a referee would give them.
  • What the Union’s own rules change: mulligans left, the cap, the breakfast ball.
  • How to put it on the card.
  • Always: guidance only, the refs and the commissioner decide.

Who can ask

  • Refs and players on the trip. The bleachers can read rulings, not ask.
  • Answers use the same Claude and the same key as the round recap: nothing new to set up.
  • A cap on questions a day keeps the cost to pennies.
