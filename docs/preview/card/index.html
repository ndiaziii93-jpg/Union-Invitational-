The flip card, three ways

C’s numbers, A’s character, and a card with two sides: the golf on the front, the web and the funny ones on the back. Tap any card to turn it over.

The figures are worked out, not invented. Six pretend rounds at Leatherhead off the White tees; every number on every card is then derived from them by the book’s own functions — the same Stableford, the same stroke allocation, the same Union handicap, the same Order of Merit.

What this is, and what it is not

Nothing leaves this page. The photograph is read by your own browser and never sent anywhere — not to the site, not to me, not to anyone. Close the tab and it is gone. If we build it for real the picture would go into the book with everything else, and that is a decision for the group rather than for me.

This is a face set into the ball, not a drawing of you. That is deliberate. The two other ways of doing it, honestly:

— A built avatar. You pick a skin tone, hair, beard, cap and so on, and the character is drawn in the same hand as the golf guy. Always on style, never a bad likeness, no photograph anywhere. Costs a drawing kit and an afternoon of picking.

— A generated character. Send the selfie to an image model and have it drawn as a cartoon caddie. Much the best likeness, and the only one that really makes you as the golf guy. But it costs money per picture, it needs a key on the server, and everybody’s face leaves the phone and goes to a company. I would not turn that on without asking the group first.

My view: ship the face in the ball, because it works today, costs nothing and keeps the photographs on the phone. Keep the generated one in your pocket for the Vegas shirts.

Then The three

Same two sides on each. Front: what he shoots. Back: the web, and the ones you would actually read out at dinner.

Last What I would still settle

Three cards in, not one. The web is noise off two rounds. I would hold it back until three finished cards and say so on the card rather than draw a shape that means nothing.
Whose card everyone can see. A nemesis hole is funny between mates; it is less funny on a page anybody can open. Likely answer: the whole card inside the book, nothing on the public site.
The caddie’s mouth. The verdict lines are mine and can be drier, or a good deal ruder.
